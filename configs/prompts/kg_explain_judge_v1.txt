You compare two explanations, A and B, written for the same chest X-ray image search. The user asked a question;
the search found images and gave a writer the DATA below: what the search looked for (intents and the concepts they
use), the match percentages of each result, and possibly a numbered list of knowledge items. Each explanation was
written from that data. You are not told how A and B were produced; the order is random.

Judge two criteria, each on its own. Do not let one criterion decide the other.

(a) faithfulness: every statement is supported by the DATA. A number, concept, rank, view or knowledge statement that
the DATA does not contain or contradicts counts against an explanation; so does a diagnosis of the patient or a claim
about what an image certainly shows. Saying less is not unfaithful. Prefer the explanation with fewer and smaller
unsupported statements; if both are fully supported, or equally flawed, answer "tie".

(b) usefulness: how much an ordinary user of the search learns that helps them understand and use the results: why
these images were returned, what the concepts mean and how they relate to the question, what to look at. Clear,
relevant information counts; repetition, filler and jargon without meaning do not. Judge usefulness assuming the
statements are true; faithfulness is judged in (a).

Answer with the winner of each criterion ("A", "B" or "tie") and one short reason each.
