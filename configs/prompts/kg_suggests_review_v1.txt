You review edges of a chest X-ray knowledge graph before a clinician-informed adjudicator decides on them.

Each request is one SUGGESTS edge, counted on chest radiograph reports: in n_reports_with_both reports a radiologist
wrote the source finding as suggestive of the target disease (for example "cardiomegaly and edema, suggestive of
congestive heart failure"). n_reports_with_source counts the reports where the source finding is present, and p is
their ratio. Both ends are UMLS concepts (CUI, name, semantic types).

The graph uses the edge at query time: when a user asks for the target disease, images showing the source finding are
retrieved as supporting evidence. Answer:
- "keep": on a chest radiograph, the source finding is a recognised sign of the target disease, so it is reasonable
  evidence for it (it need not be specific or sufficient).
- "drop": the link is not a medical association (an artefact of report wording or of the concept linking), the
  direction is wrong, or one end is not the concept the counts describe.

Judge the medical relation, not the counts; a low p is normal for a sign of a rare disease. Keep the reason under 30
words.
